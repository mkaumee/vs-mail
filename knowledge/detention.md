# Detention and demurrage

Two different charges that are often named together and frequently confused.

- **Demurrage** — the container stays *inside* the terminal beyond the free
  time. Charged by the terminal, per container per day.
- **Detention** — the container has left the terminal and is *not returned*
  empty within the free time. Charged by the carrier, per container per day.

A shipment can incur both: demurrage while it sits at the port, then detention
once it moves but the empty is returned late.

## Free time

Free time is granted per container from discharge, and is set on the booking
rather than being a fixed company policy. Typical liner terms are:

| Equipment | Free time at destination |
|---|---|
| 20' standard | 7 calendar days |
| 40' standard / high cube | 7 calendar days |
| Reefer | 3 calendar days |
| Special equipment (flat rack, open top) | 5 calendar days |

Free time runs in **calendar days including weekends and public holidays**
unless the booking says working days. Day one is the day after discharge.

## Rates

Charged per container per day, and the daily rate escalates in bands the
longer the container is held. A typical destination tariff runs in three
bands — roughly days 1-5, 6-10, then 11 onward — with the rate rising at each
step to discourage long holds.

The exact bands and amounts belong to the port tariff on the booking. Quote
the record, not a general figure.

## Confirming an amount before payment

"Kindly confirm the amount before we release payment" asks for two things: the
figure, and the basis for it. A confirmation should state the container
numbers, the days counted, the daily rate applied, and the discharge date the
count started from — because that is what makes the figure checkable.

## Disputing

Common and often legitimate grounds:

- **Terminal congestion** the consignee could not influence
- **Customs hold** not caused by the consignee's documentation
- **Carrier documentation delay** — the arrival notice or telex release came
  late, so collection could not start on time
- **Free time miscounted** — the count started from the vessel's arrival
  rather than from discharge

A dispute should be raised before payment, with the container numbers and the
dates being contested. Once paid, recovery is much harder.
